# Methodology

How every quantity in PDH Studio is computed, what it is based on and where it stops being accurate.
Source files are in `src/core/`; every statement of the form "the test suite asserts X" refers to a test you can run with `npm test`.

## 1. Symbols and traceability (`bits.ts`)

The multiplexers do not move plain bits but **symbols**: a JavaScript number whose lowest bit is the bit value and whose upper part is a *tag*. Tag 0 means "overhead generated by this multiplexer". An E1 source tags bit *n* of E1 *k* as `1 + n + k·2²⁶`. Because the multiplexers copy symbols as opaque values, the tag survives three levels of multiplexing and is decoded by `originOf` into E1 number, E1 frame, time slot (0 to 31) and bit (1 to 8).

Line errors flip only the value bit, so a corrupted bit keeps its tag and the comparison after demultiplexing can say which E1 bit was damaged.

Limit: a stuffing bit and a lower-level overhead bit that happens to occupy an S slot are both tag 0 and cannot be told apart.

## 2. PCM (`pcm.ts`, ITU-T G.711)

- 8 000 samples/s, 8 bits per sample, 64 kbit/s.
- A-law and µ-law are the segmented encoders of G.711 (13 and 15 segments), with the standard even-bit inversion for A-law (`0x55`, sign `0xD5`). `linearToAlaw(0) = 0xD5` and the full-scale codes are asserted.
- The continuous curves `F(x)` of the slides (A = 87.6, µ = 255) are plotted separately; they are the ideal characteristics the segmented codes approximate.
- SNR is **measured**: a sine is encoded, decoded and `10·log10(Σx² / Σ(x−y)²)` is computed. The uniform-quantiser reference is `6.02·n + 1.76 dB`, 49.9 dB for 8 bits.

## 3. E1 frame (`e1.ts`, ITU-T G.704, G.706, G.732)

- 32 time slots × 8 bits = 256 bits every 125 µs: 2 048 kbit/s, 30/32 = 93.75 % payload.
- **TS0.** Even frames: Si, then FAS `0011011` in bits 2 to 8. Odd frames: Si, a fixed 1 (so the word cannot imitate the FAS), the alarm bit A (RAI) and the spare bits Sa4 to Sa8.
- **TS16.** CAS: in frame 0 of the 16-frame multiframe `0000 X Y X X`; in frames 1 to 15 the ABCD nibbles of channels *i* and *i + 15*. CCS: HDLC flag `01111110` when idle.
- **CRC-4.** Generator `x⁴ + x + 1`. The CRC is the remainder of `M(x)·x⁴` over the 2 048 bits of a submultiframe with the four C bits forced to zero, and it travels in the *next* submultiframe (C1 to C4 in the Si bit of frames 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8, 10, 12, 14). The odd frames 1 to 11 carry `001011` and frames 13 and 15 carry the E bits (0 = the far end received an errored block).
  The tests assert that a codeword followed by its CRC has remainder 0, that the transmitted C bits equal the CRC of the previous submultiframe, and that a single flipped payload bit is detected in exactly one block.
- **Alignment (`findE1Alignment`).** G.706 style: FAS, then bit 2 = 1 in the next frame, then FAS again, and once more. LOF is declared after 3 consecutive wrong FAS words (or 3 consecutive wrong NFAS bit 2).
- **Line alarms.** LOS: a run of zero pulses (default 255 bit periods; G.775 allows a range, this is a configurable emulator choice). AIS: at most 2 zeros in a window of 512 bits.

## 4. Line codes (`linecode.ts`, ITU-T G.703)

- **AMI.** Ones alternate polarity.
- **HDB3.** Four zeros become `000V` or `B00V`. V repeats the polarity of the previous pulse (a deliberate violation); B follows the alternation. The choice keeps an odd number of ordinary pulses between substitutions, which bounds the running sum. Decoder: a pulse with the same polarity as the previous one is a violation; it and the three positions before it are zero.
- **B8ZS.** Eight zeros become `000VB0VB`.
- Reported: the longest zero run (decides whether the receiver keeps its clock) and the range of the running sum (DC). Both are computed on the generated pulse train, not assumed.

## 5. Justification multiplexers (`mux.ts`, ITU-T G.742 and G.751)

### Frame layouts

| | 2/8 | 8/34 | 34/140 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | 848 bits = 4 × 212 | 1 536 = 4 × 384 | 2 928 = 6 × 488 |
| Set 1 | FAS 10 + A + Sn, then 200 data | FAS 10 + A + N, then 372 data | FAS 12 + A + N + 2 reserved, then 472 data |
| Following sets | Cj1×4 / Cj2×4 / Cj3×4 + S×4 | same | Cj1×4 … Cj4×4, then Cj5×4 + S×4 |
| FAS | `1111010000` | `1111010000` | `111110100000` |
| Cj per tributary | 3 (2 of 3) | 3 (2 of 3) | 5 (3 of 5) |
| Data bits per tributary | 50 + 52 + 52 + 51 = 205 | 93 + 95 + 95 + 94 = 377 | 118 + 4 × 121 + 120 = 722 |

Data cells of a set are interleaved bit by bit among the four tributaries, restarting at tributary 1 in every set.

**What is checked and what is not.** The totals are asserted against the project slides and the recommendations: frame length, number and position count of FAS, Cj, S and data cells, 205 / 377 / 722 data bits, 1 / 1 / 1 justification bits, line rate minus four tributaries (256, 576 and 1 792 kbit/s), frame rates (9 962.26, 22 375 and 47 562.8 per second) and the stuffing ratios. The *order of the sets* and the two reserved bits of the E4 set 1 follow our reading of G.751 and are consistent with those totals; cross-check against the text of the Recommendation before relying on the position of a single control bit.

### Justification control

For tributary *t* and frame *k*:

```
t_k     = k · frame_bits / f_line                (line clock, with its ppm error)
arrived = floor(prefill + f_trib_t · t_k)         (tributary clock, with its ppm error)
level   = arrived − sent
S carries data  if  level − data_bits ≥ 1;  otherwise S is stuffing
```

The tributary is an infinite source that is *pulled*: a higher level asks the level below for bits only when it needs them, and the clock error enters through `arrived`. `prefill` is `data_bits + 2`, so the first frame never underflows.

Stuffing ratio in steady state: `ρ = (data_bits + 1) − bits_per_frame`, with `bits_per_frame = f_trib · frame_bits / f_line`. At nominal clocks this gives 0.424, 0.436 and 0.419. The tests compare the measured fraction of stuffed frames with this formula for several ppm values.

### Demultiplexing

Locate the FAS, take the data cells of each tributary, take the S cell only if the Cj majority says "data". The recovered stream is aligned to the first bit of the tributary, so comparing with a regenerated source is a straight prefix comparison.

### Errors in the control bits

A single error in a group of three (or two of five) is corrected by the vote and counted as a "Cj disagreement". Two errors in a group of three flip the decision, the tributary receives one bit too many or too few and everything after it is shifted, which the round trip counts as a burst of wrong bits.

### Limits

The loop is a threshold on the store fill: it reproduces the justification statistics and the bounded waiting-time jitter but it is not a model of a hardware phase comparator. There is no smoothing PLL at the demultiplexer, so the jitter figure is the one *before* smoothing.

## 6. Clocks and slips (`clock.ts`)

- Rate difference `Δf = rate · |ppm_A − ppm_B| · 10⁻⁶`. For an E1 with +50 and −50 ppm that is 204.8 bit/s.
- A controlled slip loses or repeats one frame of 256 bits (125 µs): `T_slip = 256 / Δf`, 1.25 s for 100 ppm of difference.
- The elastic buffer is integrated numerically: the level moves by `Δf · dt` and, when it leaves a window of one frame centred on zero, a frame is dropped or repeated. The tests count slips over 60 s and compare them with the formula.
- Phase drift (wander) after *t* seconds is `ppm_diff · 10⁻⁶ · rate · t` unit intervals of 488 ns.
- Waiting-time jitter: least-squares line through the cumulative bits delivered per frame; the peak-to-peak deviation from that line is reported in bits (unit intervals of the tributary). For a regular justification pattern it stays below 1.

## 7. Error performance (`perf.ts`, ITU-T G.826 style)

- A **block** is a CRC-4 submultiframe: 2 048 bits, 1 000 per second.
- **ES**: at least one errored block in the second. **SES**: at least 30 % of the blocks errored. **BBE**: errored blocks in seconds that are not SES. **UAS**: unavailable seconds, from the first of 10 consecutive SES until the first of 10 consecutive non-SES.
- Ratios: ESR = ES / available seconds, SESR = SES / available seconds, BBER = BBE / blocks in available seconds. A path with any unavailable time is reported as not meeting the objectives even if the ratios over its available time are good.
- Objectives used: ESR 0.04, SESR 0.002, BBER 2·10⁻⁴ (end-to-end, 1.5 to 5 Mbit/s class). These are *end-to-end* figures; a single section gets a share.
- Block error probability for a bit error ratio: `1 − (1 − BER)^2048`. The closed form for ESR and SESR sums the binomial over the 1 000 blocks of a second; the simulation draws blocks with a fixed seed. The tests require agreement.

## 8. Radio hop to E1 quality (`perf.ts`)

1. **Outage.** `U = 6·10⁻⁷ · C · (f / 1 GHz) · d³ · 10^(−FM/10)` with terrain factor *C*, distance *d* in km and fade margin FM in dB: the same Vigants-Barnett estimate used by radio-link-planner. A margin of 0 dB or less is certain outage.
2. **Clear-sky BER.** Coherent QPSK, `BER = Q(√(2·Eb/N0))`, with `Eb/N0` calibrated so that the BER is 10⁻⁶ at 0 dB margin (about 10.5 dB) and then raised by the fade margin.
3. **SES from fading.** Every second of outage is counted as severely errored, so the SESR contributed by a hop equals its outage probability; hops in series add.
4. **Budget.** The share of the 0.002 objective given to the radio is a user-chosen emulator assumption (default 10 %).

Limits: no rain, no interference, no dispersive fading; outage is an estimate for an average year, not a measurement.

## 9. Network and add/drop cost (`network.ts`, `hierarchy.ts`)

- A **trunk** is a chain of stations sharing one stream (E2, E3 or E4). A **circuit** is a number of E1 between two stations of the trunk. Capacity: 4, 16 and 64 E1.
- To drop circuits at a station, every branch of the multiplexing tree that contains a dropped slot must be demultiplexed and multiplexed back. For slots `s` on an E4, the branches are `⌊s/64⌋` at 140/34, `⌊s/16⌋` at 34/8 and `⌊s/4⌋` at 8/2. Units = 2 × (number of distinct branches over the levels); a single E1 on an E4 gives 3 + 3 = 6.
- SDH reference: one add-drop multiplexer per station.
- Added delay: each demultiplexing stage is taken to buffer about one frame of its level (E2 100.4 µs, E3 44.7 µs, E4 21.0 µs); an SDH station is counted as one E1 frame of 125 µs. This is a model, not a measurement of any equipment.
- Slot assignment strategies are described in the README; the result is reported as measured.

### Alarm propagation

For a dead link in one direction: the station downstream detects LOS, sends AIS onwards in the same direction and, if the reverse direction still works, sends RAI back (bit A of TS0). A full cut kills both directions: LOS at both ends and AIS away from the cut on both sides.

## 10. Hierarchy tables (`hierarchy.ts`)

European, North American and Japanese levels with rates and channels as in ITU-T G.702, as in the project slides. The overhead of each European level is `(line rate − 4 × tributary rate) / line rate`: 3.03 %, 1.68 % and 1.29 % (the slides round the E3 figure to 1.70 %; the test asserts the computed value).
